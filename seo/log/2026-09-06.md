## What Search Console showed

Same file as the last three runs, 718 impressions and 2 clicks across 150
queries, 2 to 29 August. The query tables are unchanged and are not repeated.

**The finding is that those 718 impressions are 25% of the site.** The page
dimension in the same file totals 2,938 impressions. Google withholds the query
on any search too rare to report, so 2,199 of them arrive with a page attached
and no query, and reach none of the four tables the report prints. The 2026-08-30
file says the same thing: 2,204 of 2,979, 74%. This is not a fetch bug and not
something a longer window fixes. It is how the API reports.

The consequence is specific and it invalidates a decision, not just a number.

**`/blog/stripe-subscription-past-due-vs-unpaid/` is 401 impressions at position
18.5, third largest page on the site.** Every run to date has seen it as its two
named rows, `stripe past due` at 4 impressions and `past_due stripe` at 4, and
the last log rejected touching it partly because "they carry 3 or 4 impressions
each, about 15 between them". The real figure is 401, of which 373 are dark. The
rejection was reasoned from a number 27 times too small.

Two more pages sit in the striking band and have never appeared in any run's
report at all, because they have no named queries whatsoever:

| impr | pos | clicks | page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 60 | 15.4 | 0 | `/blog/stripe-automatic-card-updates/` |
| 33 | 15.6 | 0 | `/blog/stripe-failed-payment-webhooks/` |

Both are 100% dark. This is the same shape of hole the last run found above
position 5, and it is three times larger.

What it does not license is a title rewrite. A dark page tells you it is losing
clicks and not what it is losing them to, so the honest use of this table is to
justify attention rather than a guess. Recorded in `ROUTINE.md` in those terms.
