RecoverFlow vs Stripe's built-in retries and dunning

By Bruce McGinley, founder of RecoverFlow · Updated July 2026

Short version: Stripe's built-in recovery is better than most people give it credit for. Smart Retries pick retry times with a machine learning model, Stripe already refuses to retry hard declines, and Stripe can email your customers about failed payments and expiring cards using your branding. Turn all of that on first. It is free. RecoverFlow is a layer on top for the parts Stripe deliberately keeps simple: control over the dunning sequence and sender, attribution showing which recovery came from which action, and history that outlives Stripe's 60 day email logs.

What Stripe already does for free

If you are comparing tools in this category, start by being clear about the baseline, because a lot of writing on this topic understates it.

Smart Retries

  • Stripe uses a machine learning model to choose when to retry a failed invoice payment, rather than a fixed interval.
  • The recommended default is 8 attempts within 2 weeks. You can set the window to 1 week, 2 weeks, 3 weeks, 1 month, or 2 months.
  • Stripe does not keep charging hard declines. On codes like lost_card, stolen_card, or revocation_of_authorization, retries wait until a new payment method appears.
  • You can switch Smart Retries off entirely and write your own retry rules.

Customer emails

  • Stripe can send a failed payment notification after a payment fails.
  • Stripe can send unpaid invoice reminders on a schedule.
  • Stripe can send a card expiration notice roughly a month before a card expires.
  • These use your Stripe branding settings, so they carry your logo and colors.

Do this first. If Smart Retries and failed payment emails are switched off in your Stripe dashboard, switch them on before you evaluate any paid tool. You may find that closes most of the gap, and that is a completely fine outcome.

Where Stripe's built-in tools stop

Stripe's recovery features are designed to be sensible defaults that work without configuration. That is the right call for Stripe, and it also means there are things they intentionally do not let you do.

  • You cannot design the sequence. There is no way to say "day 0 gentle, day 3 direct, day 7 final" with your own copy at each step.
  • You cannot control the sender. The emails come from Stripe's system, not from your domain, so they do not land in an existing thread with your customer or match the rest of your lifecycle email.
  • Attribution is thin. When an invoice eventually gets paid, it is hard to tell whether a retry did it, an email did it, or the customer's balance simply refreshed on its own.
  • Email logs are retained for 60 days. That makes any longer term analysis of what is working difficult.
  • There is no backward look. Stripe will not tell you what you lost over the last quarter and what of it was realistically recoverable.

When Stripe alone is genuinely enough

I would rather you not pay me than pay me for nothing, so here is the honest version.

  • Your failed payment volume is small. If you are losing a handful of dollars a month, no tool in this category is worth the overhead. Stripe's defaults are fine.
  • You are happy with Stripe's emails. If your customers respond to them and your recovery rate feels reasonable, there is nothing to fix.
  • You want zero extra vendors. Adding a third party to your billing path is a real decision. If the answer is no on principle, that is a legitimate answer.
  • You have engineering time and want to own it. All of this is buildable on Stripe webhooks. It is ongoing maintenance rather than a weekend, but it is not mysterious.

When the extra layer earns its place

  • Failed payments are large enough that a few percentage points of recovery is real money
  • You want your dunning to sound like you, from your address, on your schedule
  • You need to report what was recovered and why, past a 60 day window
  • You want to know the size of the problem before you spend anything on it

How the two fit together

RecoverFlow is not a replacement for Stripe and does not turn Stripe's features off. Stripe still processes every charge and still holds every card. RecoverFlow connects through Stripe Connect with access you grant and can revoke from your own Stripe dashboard, and never sees card numbers.

The attribution point is the one that matters most for cost. Because Stripe recovers some payments on its own, and some customers pay on their own, a recovery tool that bills on results has to be careful about what it takes credit for. RecoverFlow only bills on recoveries it can attribute to a specific action it took. Anything that came back on its own is not billed. That is deliberately the conservative direction, and you can read the full detail on the pricing page.

Frequently asked questions

Does Stripe already retry failed subscription payments?

Yes. Smart Retries use a machine learning model to choose retry times, with a recommended default of 8 attempts within 2 weeks, and the window is configurable between 1 week and 2 months. Stripe also stops retrying hard declines until a new payment method is added. It is a genuinely capable free feature and you should have it switched on.

Does Stripe send dunning emails?

Yes. Stripe can send failed payment notifications, unpaid invoice reminders, and card expiration notices about a month before a card expires, using your Stripe branding settings. What you cannot do is control the sequence, the timing between messages, the sender, or the copy.

So why would I need anything besides Stripe?

Often you would not, and if Stripe's defaults are working for you it is reasonable to stop there. The gaps that matter are control over the dunning sequence and sender, attribution showing which recovery came from which action, and history that outlives Stripe's 60 day email log retention.

Does RecoverFlow replace Stripe's retries?

No. It runs alongside Stripe rather than replacing it. Stripe still processes every charge and still holds every card.

How do I find out whether I have a problem worth solving?
